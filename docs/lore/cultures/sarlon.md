# Sarlon · culture guide

**Status: approved by the owner, 2026-11-13.** See [the overview](the-seven.md) for how to read this.

## In one line

A vast plateau kingdom of wheat, sheep and quarrelsome lords, whose northern knights rule a city the Rabene built and kept its stars, baths and gardens.

> “a vast land-kingdom of dry plateaus, wheat, sheep and windmills, studded with the square tower-castles of its innumerable quarrelsome lords” — `realm_sarlon`

> “taken from the Rabene governors three centuries ago by northern knights who kept the glazed-brick palaces, the terraced gardens, the star-priests and the bath-houses, and added keeps of their own” — `realm_sarlon`

## Names

Sarlon has **two** naming traditions, and canon keeps them apart.

**Sarlonese people in canon:** Rowan III, Gonzalo de Montaraz, Inés de Vela, Álvaro de Ribadoro, Fernán Osorio, Paloma Ruiz, Teodoro Alcón, Gonzalo Ferrer. Abroad: Baltasar de Ocampo, Íñigo Salcedo, Rodrigo de Alcaraz.

**Rabene people in canon (Tarsena and the court's stargazers):** Nabu-iddin, Mar-Ishtu, Ninsar Bar-Parod, Shalumtu, Shamhat bat-Ilu.

**Places in canon:** Valmonte, the Plains of Torrecas, Torrealba, Castroviejo, Tarsena, the Peñagris Highlands, Sotterra, the Colossi of Vérangue, the Wolf's Gate, the Great Drove-Road.

**Patterns:**
- **Sarlonese nobles** are given name + `de` + the house: Gonzalo de Montaraz. The house is `House` + name: House Montaraz, House Ribadoro.
- **Sarlonese commoners** have a given name and one family name, no `de`: Paloma Ruiz, Fernán Osorio.
- **Accents are part of the name:** Inés, Álvaro, Íñigo, Fernán, Peñagris.
- **Rabene names** are hyphened or carry a parent: `Bar-` (son of) and `bat-` (daughter of): Ninsar Bar-Parod, Shamhat bat-Ilu. Others are one name: Shalumtu.
- **Places** are plateau compounds: *Torre-alba* (white tower), *Castro-viejo* (old fort), *Val-monte*. Plain-speech names mark the frontier and the roads: the Wolf's Gate, the Great Drove-Road, the Inn of the Two Windmills.

## Titles and ranks

- **King**, then **Count** and **Marquess**, each with a house and a tower-castle.
- **The Free Lances:** Caravan Guard, Hired Lance, Free Lance, Corporal of Lances, Captain of Free Lances.
- **A noble household** (Montaraz): Page, Squire, Household Knight, Champion. (Ribadoro, a wool house): Wool-Clerk, Toll-Keeper, Steward of the Flocks, Chamberlain.
- **The Royal Garrison:** Recruit, Pikeman of the Gate, Sergeant of the Gate, Lieutenant of the Gate.
- **The Royal Observatory:** Instrument-Boy, Computer, Observer, Astronomer Royal's Deputy. The court keeps a Royal Astronomer and Star-Readers, who are Rabene.
- **The Rabene of Tarsena** answer to a **Consul**, who is also a chief of Parod's lodge.
- **The sheep-owners** are the Honourable Council of the Flocks, "masters of the drove-roads and their laws".

## Faiths

Five faiths have a site here, more than any realm but Eintenn, and they split along the same line as the names.

- **Saint Calder** (knights) and **Dell** (the court's lamp-chapel) are the northern faiths.

> “Dell’s chapel at court, where lamps veiled in pierced brass throw stars on the walls and vespers are sung in five tongues.” — `sarlon_city/lamp`

- **Ulna of the Deep Earth** belongs to the gem-working highlands.

> “Grey crags and thin pasture above the western plains, honeycombed with gem-workings.” — `sarlon_highlands`

- **Laishma, Mother of Monsters**, and **Parod the Storm-Chief** are the Rabene gods of Tarsena.

## Food and drink

Canon dishes: **Olla of the Plateau**, **Saffron Lamb of Valmonte**, **Drovers' Roast Lamb**, **Shepherd's Migas**, **Plateau Ham**, **Caravan Flatbread**, **Sarlonese Sack**; and from the Rabene quarter, **Rabene Date-Beer** and **Tarsena Date-Cakes**.

> “Liars, poets and lunatics, and decent wine.” — `sarlon_plains/inn`

> “Date-beer houses, clay-tablet scribes and seal-cutters, where Rabena is still the first language.” — `tarsena/quarter`

Sheep, wheat and saffron on the plateau; dates and beer by the sea.

## Dress

Wool is the kingdom's wealth, so cloth is politics.

> “a wool-town of shearing-sheds, dye-works, poets and toll-booths straddling the Great Drove-Road” — `castroviejo`

The one person canon dresses is the Rabene consul: "beautifully dressed", bearded.

**Canon is silent** on what Sarlonese people wear. **Proposed:** dyed merino as the mark of standing, a house's colours on its household, and for the Rabene of Tarsena, beards, seals worn on the person, and richer cloth than the knights think proper.

## Oaths and sayings

Sarlon is a land of knights and of talkers.

> “Sarlon’s roads are famous for knights-errant, its inns for liars, and its army for winning wars it has no particular need to fight.” — `realm_sarlon`

**Canon is silent** on the words of an oath. **Proposed:** knights swear on Calder and on their house ("on Montaraz", "by my lance"); Rabene swear by Parod's bull or not at all; and every inn has a boast that begins "It is well known that…".

## What people value

- **Never yielding.**

> “Old banners, a long table of knights and a count who has never retreated.” — `torrealba/keep`

- **Old blood over new money,** and the resentment that follows.

> “They are cleverer than the Montaraz and richer, and have never been forgiven for either.” — `castroviejo`

- **A court of many tongues.** The king rules "over a court of five languages", and the stars are read by Rabene.
- **Reach.** Sarlon sends its chancellors to govern Grell and watches the Diet closely.

## Rules for new names here

- Decide first: Sarlonese or Rabene. Do not mix the two in one name.
- Sarlonese nobles: given name + `de` + house. Commoners: no `de`. Keep the accents.
- Rabene: hyphened names, or `Bar-` / `bat-` + a parent's name. Rabene belong in Tarsena, the Observatory and the consulate unless there is a reason.
- Places: plateau compounds (*Torre-*, *Castro-*, *Val-*, *Peña-*). Do not reuse Montaraz, Ribadoro, Torrealba, Castroviejo or Valmonte.
