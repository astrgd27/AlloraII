# Eintenn · culture guide

**Status: draft, waiting for the owner's approval.** See [the overview](the-seven.md) for how to read this.

## In one line

A small, rich duchy on the northern coast, held together by feudal oaths and paid for by salt, silver and harbour-tolls.

> “Smallest of the Seven Principalities and among the richest, Eintenn clings to the northern coast between the Grauwald and the sea.” — `realm_eintenn`

> “barons hold their manors of him, knights hold of the barons, and villeins owe labour on the demesne three days in seven” — `realm_eintenn`

## Names

**People in canon:** Albrecht IV von Tennfels, Margarethe von Tennfels, Hanne Brook, Jory Pell, Sir Odo of Hartsmoor, Mechthild Ambrosius, Mother Brindl.

**Places in canon:** Ostwick, Kesselhaven, Burg Tennfels, Aschau, the Grauwald, the Hohenkamm, Silberhalde, the Salzmoor, Pomerell, the Salzmarkt.

**Patterns:**
- **Nobles** take northern given names and `von` + their seat: Albrecht von Tennfels.
- **Knights** are `Sir` + a given name + `of` + a place: Sir Odo of Hartsmoor.
- **Common folk** have a short given name and a short, plain family name: Hanne Brook, Jory Pell.
- **Scholars** may carry a learned, old-tongue family name: Mechthild Ambrosius.
- **Wise-women and elders** are `Mother` + a name: Mother Brindl.
- **Places** are built from northern roots joined into one word: *Grau-wald* (grey wood), *Salz-moor*, *Silber-halde*, *Hohen-kamm*, *Kessel-haven*, *Tenn-fels*. Villages can end in *-wick* or *-au*: Ostwick, Aschau.
- **Plain-speech names** sit beside them: The Barley Sheaf, The Gilded Eel, The Stone Mole, The Thunder-Caves, The Ribboned Oaks.

## Titles and ranks

- The ruler is the **Duke**, called **the Salt-Duke**; his daughter is the **Heiress**.

> “Duke Albrecht IV von Tennfels, called the Salt-Duke, rules from Burg Tennfels through a web of feudal oaths” — `realm_eintenn`

- **The Duke's men:** Levy Spearman, Man-at-Arms, Sergeant, Serjeant-at-Arms, Captain of the Castle Guard.
- **The land:** Farmhand, Shepherd, Ploughman, Hayward, Master Husbandman. The village answers to a reeve.

> “Long furrowed strips of the three-field rotation, worked in common under the reeve’s eye.” — `ostwick/fields`

- **The guilds of Kesselhaven** are named for a tool: the Adze (carpenters), the Anvil (smiths), the Needle (tailors, weavers, tanners), the Book (scribes and binders). Above them sit the merchants of the Salt Council and the Mariners' Guild.

> “ringed by the guildhalls of the Adze, the Anvil, the Needle and the Book” — `kess_market`

- **Learning:** Copyist, Scribe of the Collegium, Tutor, Lector, Magister. The mages are the Hermetic Order: Famulus, Adept-Warder, Magus, Magus of the Inner Circle.
- **Below the law:** the Rats of the Undercroft: Lookout, Cutpurse, Burglar, Fence, Smuggler-Captain, Master Thief.

## Faiths

Eintenn has more shrines than any other realm of the Seven: nine faiths have a site here.

- **Ilvara of the Two Flames** is the village faith: a hearth in every village.

> “A round stone shrine by the well where the village fire never goes out.” — `ostwick/shrine`

- **Saint Calder the Oathbound** is the knights' saint, and **Dell** has a lamp-chapel at court.

> “The chapel of Saint Calder the Oathbound holds the banners of a hundred sworn knights, and in a quiet side-court the legate of the Dellian Diet keeps a lamp-chapel to Dell.” — `tennfels`

- **Orthun Stormfather** is kept in the high places by the Cave-Sworn.

> “The Cave-Sworn keep their silent vigils here, waiting for the storm.” — `thunder_caves`

- Also here: Mother Vass of the Deep (the harbour's Drowned Chapel), Horro, the Greenwife (the Ribboned Oaks), Norrae, and the Veiled One (the Threshold Sanctum, beneath Old Vaelis).

## Food and drink

Canon dishes: **Salt-Duke's Herring**, **Ostwick Honey-Cake**. Ale in the village, wine from Aschau, and everything else through the harbour.

> “salt, silver and stockfish go out, wine, spice and glass come in” — `kess_harbor`

> “A vine-town on the warm southern slopes, famous for its wine and infamous for its fields.” — `aschau`

> “Wattle-and-daub cottages, each with its kitchen garden, bread-oven and loom.” — `ostwick/cottages`

## Dress

**Canon is silent.** Nothing in Eintenn's places or people describes what anyone wears. The only hints are trades: the Guild of the Needle covers "tailors, weavers, tanners and furriers", and cottages keep a loom.

**Proposed:** plain wool and linen for villagers, with the guild's tool as a badge for guildsmen, and the ducal eagle (the realm's emblem in Allora I) on the Duke's men. Sumptuary laws exist in canon ("within the sumptuary laws", the Tailoring skill), so dress should mark rank.

## Oaths and sayings

Oaths are the realm's backbone: land is held by oath, and knights are sworn.

> “the banners of a hundred sworn knights” — `tennfels`

One saying is canon, from an eel-catcher:

> “Never eat an eel you didn’t kill yourself,” — `jory`

**Canon is silent** on everyday oaths and curses. **Proposed:** people swear by what the realm lives on and by its gods: "by salt and silver", "by the hearth" (Ilvara), "on Calder's oath". Storm-oaths ("Orthun hear me") belong to the high country.

## What people value

- **Holding land of your own**, however small.

> “One day I’ll have my own strip by the river,” — `hanne`

- **Rank kept and oaths kept.** The whole realm is a ladder of who holds of whom.
- **The towns want what Luggen has.**

> “whose burghers chafe under ducal taxes and envy the liberties of the merchant-princes of Luggen” — `realm_eintenn`

- **Shrewdness in the old.** The Duke is admired for being hard to kill, and the Heiress for already doing the real business.

> “it is said in Kesselhaven that the Salt-Council already brings its real business to her” — `ppl_margarethe`

## Rules for new names here

- Villages and manors: northern compound words (wood, moor, ford, hill, stone roots) or *-wick*, *-au*, *-haven*, *-fels*, *-wald*. Do not reuse Tennfels, Grauwald, Salzmoor, Silberhalde, Hohenkamm or Hartsmoor.
- Nobles: `von` + a new seat. Knights: `Sir` + name + `of` + place.
- Common folk: one short given name, one short plain family name.
- Inns, quays and shrines: plain speech with `The`.
